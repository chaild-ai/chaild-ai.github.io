On Saturday 26 September we hosted [Celebrating Youth Voices in AI & Data Science](/blog/youth-voices-showcase-2026/) in the Dining Hall of Reuben College, Oxford, to mark the end of a year of the MPLS youth advisory group. Around 40 teenagers and parents came, along with two MPLS researchers who had taken part in one of the youth advisory group sessions we facilitated earlier in the year. From the CHAILD team, Jun, Isobel, Vid, Manolis and Yildiz hosted the session.

![Rows of tables in the Reuben College Dining Hall under its dark timber roof arches, with teenagers and parents seated facing a screen showing the welcome slide](/content/blog/images/youth-voices-2026-hall.jpg "The Reuben College Dining Hall, just after the welcome")

## The morning

After a welcome and an introduction to the CHAILD project, the morning followed the agenda below:

- **Youth advisory group summary and reflections**, led by Isobel. A look back over the year: the [kick-off meeting](/blog/youth-advisory-group-kickoff/) in February, our own session [co-designing stories about generative AI and learning](/blog/youth-advisory-group-2/), sessions with other Oxford researchers on quantum computing outreach, genomics and AI for rare cancer care, and the Zooniverse citizen science platform, and finally the wrap-up meeting. Group members and researchers then reflected together on why it matters for young people and researchers to work together on new technology.
- **Youth co-design study findings**, led by Vid. Over two rounds of co-design sessions, 32 young people produced 41 designs in the first round and 19 of them (9 returning) produced another 25 in the second, which we turned into 7 prototypes along the way.
- **Teenagers' lightning reports** on what they took away from the experience, which research activities they took part in, and what they enjoyed.
- **An introduction to the afternoon breakout sessions**, followed by lunch.

![Dr Jun Zhao at a lectern beside a large screen showing the event title slide, with the logos of the University of Oxford, Reuben College, UCL Knowledge Lab, the Institute for Ethics in AI and UK Research and Innovation](/content/blog/images/youth-voices-2026-welcome.jpg "Opening the day")

Here is some of what youth advisory group members wrote about their year:

> "I think that I have grown more confident [in] sharing my ideas with others as this group has been very supportive and listens to your views."

> "I think that I am more confident in using AI and knowing that I will still be able to learn, and not let it take over the entire learning process, whilst still being a valuable tool."

> "I feel I've seen many aspect[s] of professions and broadened my view on certain careers and how AI affects them."

> "I think I've gained greater insight on how other people my age think and what they prioritise in importance. This experience has also helped me understand the different processes that happen when collecting data and how methods like mind maps and categorising can help in drawing out specific trends from a data set."

## The afternoon

In the afternoon we split into roundtables, which ran in two sessions either side of a tea break:

- **Parents on generative AI**: what do you think your child uses AI for, and what concerns you about it?
- **Parents on the DfE standards**: how should generative AI do better for children's cognitive development, and what worries you about manipulation?
- **Young people on the DfE standards**: going through the use cases and then marking up printed copies of the standards in green (agree) and red (disagree), with notes on why.
- **Young people on new AI tools**: trying out work-in-progress prototypes we built from the findings of our co-design workshops. This round of prototypes took its inspiration from Real Chat AI, a tool created by Neontribe (now part of dxw) to help young people use generative AI thoughtfully. You can read about how they developed it in [their blog post](https://www.dxw.com/2025/05/delivering-a-way-to-help-young-people-use-generative-ai-thoughtfully-the-solution/).

The standards in question are the Department for Education's [Generative AI: product safety standards](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/generative-ai-product-safety-expectations), which were updated in January this year with new sections on cognitive development, emotional and social development, mental health, and manipulation. These are the areas where CHAILD's work on children's agency is most relevant, so it was valuable to hear what the people they are meant to protect make of them.

![Teenagers around a table covered with printed screen templates, sticky notes and marker pens, sketching on paper, with a laptop open on a prototype called Real Chat AI](/content/blog/images/youth-voices-2026-breakout.jpg "Young people testing and sketching on our prototypes")

## What we heard

A few messages came through clearly across the day:

- **For some parents, it was the first time they had stopped to think about how their children actually use AI tools.** The parent roundtables gave them space to compare notes, and to raise questions they hadn't had a chance to ask before.
- **Young people** shared even more new creative ideas for how AI tools could support them in being intentional and deliberate.
- **Parents and young people both had a say on the DfE standards**, and on our prototypes. Feedback on both will feed into how we refine our tools for the next research study.
