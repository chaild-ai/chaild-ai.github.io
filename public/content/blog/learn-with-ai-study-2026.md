Researchers at University College London and the University of Oxford are investigating how young people aged 13–17 can stay in charge of their own learning when learning with AI. If you are the parent or guardian of a teenager, we would love for them to take part — please [register their interest](https://forms.cloud.microsoft/e/1fwKt0MPrw).

## Why we're doing this

As generative AI becomes part of everyday learning, there is growing concern that young people may come to rely on it in ways that affect their agency. Building on our [co-design work with young people](/blog/youth-voices-showcase-recap-2026/), we have developed a learning application that helps them set their own goals, reflect on their learning, and make their own choices, rather than letting the AI decide for them.

We want to find out how using it shapes young people's sense of agency, and how they act on it, over time; what prompts those changes; and how well our measures of agency match what young people actually do and tell us about their experience.

## What taking part involves

Over five weeks, your child uses our web app for learning of their own choosing, whenever it suits them. After parental consent and their own agreement to take part, they will:

- fill in a short questionnaire about agency and a demographic questionnaire;
- join an introductory session in week 1;
- use the app in their everyday learning, with their interactions logged, in weeks 1–5;
- take part in short, roughly weekly interviews and check-in surveys about how it's going;
- fill in a closing questionnaire and have a closing interview of about 20–30 minutes in week 5.

At the end of the study, we will also invite parents to an informal conversation or focus group of about an hour, in person or on Microsoft Teams.

Schools can take part too. Any subjects or tasks are agreed with teachers so that they fit normal teaching, and nothing is assessed.
